# Vocabulary coverage of Unit 1 (Lessons 1–15) vs. the 1,000 most common Italian words

*8 Nov 2026: all of Unit 1 (Lessons 1–15, after the playtest reworks) and all 12 Lesson 12 quests. First run (6 Nov, through Lesson 14): 201 taught.*

## The reference list

Alma's *Le prime 1000 parole italiane* (R. Tartaglione) is a printed exercise book; its word list isn't published online, so it couldn't be used directly. As the closest open equivalent this uses the **Nuovo vocabolario di base** (De Mauro & Chiari, 2016, CC BY 4.0): its *fondamentale* tier is the ~2,000 words that make up roughly 90% of what Italians say and write. Those 2,000 were ranked by real-world frequency (the `wordfreq` corpus, all inflected forms of each word added together) and the **top 1,000** kept. That list leans a little more towards news and the internet than a textbook would (*governo, sito, presidente* rank high), so the theme lists below keep only words an A1–A2 learner needs.

## What counts as covered

- **Taught:** the word (in any form) is in the player's dictionary after Lessons 1–15 and all 12 quests: tiles, Vocabolario, the quests' *schede*, set phrases, the numbers.
- **Seen only:** it turns up in the game's Italian (NPC lines, handbook examples) at least twice, but is never taught.
- **Missing:** neither.

## Unit 2 priorities

Since the first run, Unit 1 has picked up the connectors (*ma, perché, quando, quindi, se, anche, così, dopo*), *molto, poco,
qui, chiedere, famiglia, imparare*, and Venice's *mare, linea, canale*. The core verbs of moving around and asking (*andare,
venire, tornare, uscire, entrare, partire, arrivare, aspettare, cercare, portare, aiutare, trovare, capire, sapere*) are all
taught. What's left falls into six priorities, roughly in order of how often Italians use the words:

1. **The words that carry grammar (top 300, almost all "seen only").** The possessives *tuo, suo, nostro, loro, vostro*
   (only *mio* is taught); *noi, voi, li*; *tutto, altro, stesso, solo, ogni, qualche, qualcuno, primo, ultimo*; *dare*.
   And the imperfect: *era* (rank 51) and *c'era* are among the most common words in Italian, and *stato* (been) too.
   These are A2 grammar: possessives, the imperfect (*quando ero piccolo…*), and object pronouns.
2. **Everyday verbs.** *pensare* (*penso che…*), *credere*, *mettere*, *lasciare*, *diventare*, *vivere / abitare*, *rimanere /
   restare*, *usare*, *iniziare / cominciare*, *lavorare*, *cambiare*, *provare*, *perdere*, *decidere*, *scegliere*,
   *conoscere*, *succedere*, *incontrare*, *dimenticare*. *Leggere, scrivere, pagare, chiudere, aprire, dormire, studiare* are
   seen but never taught.
3. **The calendar.** *settimana, mese*, the days and months (none taught), *data, età* (how old are you?), *stagione, estate,
   inverno*, *inizio / fine*, *scorso / prossimo*, and how often: *spesso, ogni giorno, mai*.
4. **Family, home, work and money.** *casa, lavoro, padre, madre, figlio / figlia, fratello / sorella, marito / moglie,
   genitori, gente*; *ufficio, scuola, medico, professore*; *soldi, prezzo, costo, spesa*.
5. **Opinions and describing.** *importante, possibile, difficile / facile, interessante, migliore / meglio / peggio, famoso,
   utile, brutto, libero*; *idea, esempio, domanda / risposta, motivo, problema* (seen); *vero, diverso, nuovo* (seen).
6. **The world, the body, feelings.** *mondo, paese, lingua, straniero, francese, tedesco, americano, isola, cielo, centro,
   nord / sud, campagna*; *occhio, mano, cuore, corpo, faccia*; *paura, amore, voglia* (*avere voglia di*).

Left out on purpose: news and politics (*governo, politica, legge, pubblico, sistema*), the internet (*sito, video,
articolo*), and slang.

### Where they could go in Venice (ideas)
- **Checking in at the hotel:** the calendar (*per tre notti, dal 10 al 13 novembre*), *nome, età, data*, the room (*la
  camera, la chiave*), possessives (*il mio passaporto, la sua camera*).
- **Dinner at Bepi's (his family):** family words, *lavoro, casa, abitare, vivere*, possessives (*mio figlio, sua moglie*),
  *gente, insieme*.
- **Bepi's memories of old Venice:** the imperfect (*era, c'era, quando ero giovane…*), *diventare, cambiare, rimanere,
  ultimo, primo*.
- **Lost in the calli:** *perdere* (*mi sono perso!*), *dentro / fuori, intorno, insieme, mettere*, the map again.
- **Murano and Burano:** *isola, cielo, colori*, opinions (*penso che, credo che, importante, interessante, migliore*),
  superlatives.
- **The Rialto market at dawn:** *soldi, prezzo, spesa*, fish, *scegliere, decidere*.
- **At the pharmacy or the doctor:** the body, *mi fa male, dolore, medico*.
- **Planning the rest of the week:** the future (*domani andremo…*), the days, *spesso, ogni*.
- **Bepi, Margaret and Biscuit as recurring characters**, with the first lessons as expanded reviews (the designer's plan).
  Those review lessons are a good place to bring in the "seen only" words of priority 1 cheaply.

## Files and rerunning

- `docs/data/core1000-coverage.csv`: all 1,000 words with rank, part of speech, status, and how often each is taught and seen.
- `scripts/vocab/`: the pipeline. `scrape_dict.js` opens the built game with every lesson and quest done and reads the dictionary; `analyze.py` compares it with the reference list (`fo_ranked_unique.json`); `report.py` makes the theme lists.

  ```
  node scripts/build.js
  node scripts/vocab/scrape_dict.js $PWD/dist/index.html /tmp/dict.json 15
  cd scripts/vocab && python3 analyze.py ../.. /tmp/dict.json /tmp/result.json   # needs: pip install wordfreq simplemma
  ```
- **Caveats:**
  - Lemmatising is automatic, so a few words may be miscounted either way.
  - "Seen" comes from scanning Italian text in the source code, which can include lines a given player never reaches.
