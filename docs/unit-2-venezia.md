# Unit 2 · Venezia — design doc (draft)

> Drafted with the designer on 8 Nov 2026. Venice as an open world: districts that open one by one, a few structured
> lessons in each, and many quests to choose from, so learners build the vocabulary that interests them. A separate game
> (its own build and artifact) that assumes Unit 1 is done, on the same shared engine.

## The shape
- **4 districts**, opened in order:
  1. San Marco and Castello
  2. San Polo, Santa Croce and Dorsoduro
  3. Cannaregio
  4. La Giudecca
- **Each district has a time and a theme.** It runs through its main lessons, and its quests keep coming back to it (the
  designer: "each district should focus on an event in time that the other lessons reference").
- **Per district (the designer, 8 Nov):** a player goes through about 10 lessons, so about 40 for the unit:
  - **3 main lessons:** structured like Unit 1. Story and grammar; the first district's are also expanded reviews of Unit 1.
  - **About 10 quests to choose from:** each with its own topic and words, and more involved than Lesson 12's (several
    steps, several people, longer conversations, a small story).
  - **The gate:** the 3 main lessons plus **7 of the quests** open the next district (3 + 7 = about 10 lessons per
    district). The other quests stay available, and the player picks which 7 by interest.
- **Words:**
  - **Main lessons:** at most 50 words each in the Vocabolario (the cap stays).
  - **Quests:** up to 20 words and phrases each.
  - **Over the unit:** a player who does the 12 main lessons and 28 quests meets at most 12 × 50 + 28 × 20, about 1,160
    entries. With overlaps the target is **just under 1,000 words met**, with **a strong grasp of about 500**. A player
    who does every quest meets more.
  - **What that needs:** the core 500 must come back again and again. Later quests reuse earlier words on purpose, and
    Studio reviews the words of finished quests (spaced review).
  - **Which words:** docs/vocab-coverage.md's Unit 2 priorities decide which words are core.
- **Day and night only when the story calls for it.** No running clock: the night falls when an event triggers it, for
  example a friend invites you out and you stay out all night, or the Redentore fireworks. Unit 1's night lighting is reused.
- **Not Stardew's systems** (no farming, money economy or calendar). Stardew's open-world feel is the model: walk anywhere
  that's open, go into shops and homes, meet regulars.
- **Trust storylines.** Some characters have a run of quests. Help them three times and they trust you: an invitation to
  dinner at their flat, a key to a garden, a ride in their boat. Each storyline is also a vocabulary arc (family at the
  dinner, boats on the ride).

## The districts

### 1 · San Marco and Castello: **Com'era** (the past: the imperfect)
- **Theme:** what Venice used to be like. Old photos, the Doge's city, Bepi's childhood in Castello, the Arsenale's shipyards,
  floods remembered (*l'acqua alta del '66*).
- **Grammar:** the imperfect (*era, c'era, c'erano, avevo, andavo, facevamo*); *quando ero piccolo…*; the imperfect vs the
  passato prossimo (background vs event). Possessives (*mio, tuo, suo, nostro, loro*) and the calendar (days, months,
  dates, ages) come up here too, since the first main lessons double as expanded reviews.
- **Main lessons:**
  1. **The hotel.** Check in, with dates, nights, the room, your name and age. Review of Unit 1, plus the calendar and
     possessives.
  2. **Bepi's Venice.** Bepi shows you his old Castello: *qui c'era…, quando ero giovane…*. The imperfect.
  3. **The Doge's city.** A guided visit, or old photographs at the Correr, describing what it was like. The imperfect vs the
     passato prossimo.
- **Quests (ideas):** a lost glove at Florian's · Margaret's grandmother's old photo (where was it taken?) · the gondolier's
  workshop (*squero*) · a mask shop · the Arsenale's old sailor · pigeons and tourists in the piazza · the bell-ringer of
  San Zaccaria · a postcard home · the clockmaker of the Torre dell'Orologio · Biscuit runs off in the calli.
- **Trust storyline: Bepi** (Castello). Three quests, then dinner at his flat with his family: family words, *lavoro*,
  *abitare / vivere*, *mio figlio, sua moglie*.

### 2 · San Polo, Santa Croce and Dorsoduro: **Cosa sta succedendo?** (the present: *stare* + gerund)
- **Theme:** the city at work right now. The Rialto market at dawn, students at Ca' Foscari, restorers at the Accademia,
  the boatmen, an *acqua alta* morning (sirens, raised walkways, boots).
- **Grammar:** *sto cercando, sta piovendo, stanno arrivando*; *mentre*; describing what people are doing; reflexives in
  daily life (*mi alzo, si chiama*, revisited).
- **Main lessons:**
  1. **Crossing the Rialto.** The market: what's happening, who's doing what.
  2. **Acqua alta.** The sirens go, and the city adapts while you help people (*sta salendo l'acqua!*).
  3. **At the Accademia.** Restorers at work; describing what you see happening in a painting.
- **Quests (ideas):** the fish seller's missing delivery · a student who needs a flat-share ad read · the bookshop with the
  gondola full of books · a rower training for the regatta · the *cicchetti* bar crawl · a lost cat in Campo Santa
  Margherita · the mask maker's order · a tourist who missed the boat · the pharmacist (the body: *mi fa male…*) · the
  shoemaker.
- **Trust storyline:** the Rialto fruit seller. Three quests, then a ride on her *topa* (boat) to the islands' gardens: boats,
  food, *scegliere / decidere*.

### 3 · Cannaregio: **Secondo me…** (opinions)
- **Theme:** what Venetians think. The quiet residential city: the Ghetto's history, the Fondamenta della Misericordia in
  the evening, locals and tourism, the city's future debated over a spritz.
- **Grammar:** *secondo me, per me, penso di sì / di no, credo che…, trovo che…, sono d'accordo / non sono d'accordo*,
  comparatives and superlatives (*il più bello, il migliore*), *perché* with reasons.
  - **The subjunctive:** at A2 it's taught only as fixed chunks (*penso che sia…*); the full forms are for later.
- **Main lessons:**
  1. **At the Misericordia.** A table of locals argues about the best *bacaro*, so give your opinion.
  2. **Venice for Venetians.** Residents on tourism: agree, disagree, explain why.
  3. **The art debate.** A gallery opening, where you say what you think of the work.
- **Quests (ideas):** a nonna's recipe dispute · choosing a gift for Margaret · judging a cicchetti contest · the record
  shop owner's top three · a survey for a student project · which bridge is the most beautiful · a neighbour's noisy
  dog · two friends who disagree about a film · a rowing club vote · the baker's new pastry.
- **Trust storyline:** a nonna in the Ghetto. Three quests, then she teaches you her family recipe in her kitchen: the
  kitchen, quantities, opinions about food (*secondo me ci vuole più sale!*).

### 4 · La Giudecca: **Domani** (the future, hopes and plans)
- **Theme:** what will happen. The Redentore festival (the third Sunday of July, with fireworks the night before; the votive bridge of boats, the
  fireworks over the water); the island's workshops and gardens; the city's future (the sea, the MOSE barriers, young people staying or
  leaving).
- **Grammar:** the future (*sarà, andrò, ci saranno*), *spero di…, vorrei…, un giorno…, se + present → future*, plans
  (*sabato andiamo…*).
- **Main lessons:**
  1. **Preparing the Redentore.** Plans and the future: who will do what, and when.
  2. **Hopes.** The people you've met tell you their hopes for the city and for themselves; you tell yours.
  3. **The fireworks.** The finale: everyone you've helped on the boats, and a goodbye to Margaret and Biscuit
     (*ci vediamo l'anno prossimo!*).
- **Quests (ideas):** the boat decorations · the festival menu · a fisherman's weather forecast · the community garden ·
  an artist's studio opening next week · the regatta entry · a young couple deciding whether to stay in Venice · the
  ferry timetable · a letter to your future self · inviting everyone to the boat.
- **Trust storyline:** a family boatbuilder. Three quests, then you're on their boat for the fireworks.

## Recurring characters
- **Margaret and Biscuit** (the hotel is theirs too): they appear in each district, Margaret needs translations, and Biscuit
  causes small chaos.
- **Bepi:** the guide to old Venice, with his family in Castello.
- **New regulars:** each district's storyline character, plus a few faces who move around (the gondolier, the waiter at
  the hotel bar).

## Quests as data (the scalability requirement)
The designer: adding a quest "should look like just adding NPCs and dialogue and vocab and pointing them to existing
attributes". So a quest is a data file. The engine provides the building blocks, and a new quest only combines them:
- **Who and where:** NPCs (look, place, register Lei/tu, where they are by day and by night), and the places.
- **Words:** the quest's card set (its *scheda*) and the tile groups for its conversations.
- **Steps, from a fixed set of types:**
  - `talk`: a conversation, with expected meanings → replies, and a next step.
  - `go`: reach a place.
  - `give` / `get`: bag items.
  - `listen`: a heard line plus a notepad answer such as a time, price or number.
  - `translate`: for Margaret, typed or picked.
  - `choose`: a choice.
  - `look`: Vedo vedo / describe a picture.
  - `cutscene`.
  - `night`: an event that turns the evening into night (an invitation out, the fireworks).
- **Conversations:** written as *expected meanings* (intents and phrases) with the reply to each. The engine supplies the
  rest:
  - the generic answers (*Ancora una volta*, Sì / No, greetings, goodbyes in the right register);
  - hints that never give the answer (`hintAfter`);
  - the dictionary check.
- **Requirements and rewards:**
  - **Requires:** a district, a main lesson, other quests, a trust level with someone.
  - **Rewards:** trust, a stamp, an item, an unlock (a door, a place, a storyline step).
- **Tests are generated from the data:** every quest is played through the real UI by a generic test, so a new quest is
  tested without new test code.

Unit 1's quests (Lesson 12) were each written as code. Unit 2 needs the building blocks first, then the content.

## The world engine
- **The map:** a walkable grid for each district, greatly simplified, bounded by the Grand Canal and the lagoon. Campi,
  calli, bridges and fondamenta, stored as map data (tile maps).
- **Loading zones:** neighbourhoods load as you walk, and doors lead to interiors (shops, bars, homes, churches).
- **The camera** follows the player; the map shows open and closed districts and quest markers. Fast travel goes by
  vaporetto stops.
- **Gates:** the bridges (Rialto, Accademia) open with the district's gate. Giudecca is reached by vaporetto. Cannaregio is
  on the San Marco side of the Grand Canal, so its gate is a footbridge closed for repairs or a guide who takes you there.
- **Saves:** separate from Unit 1's; the game starts as though Unit 1 is done.

## Settled (8 Nov)
- **Size:** 3 main lessons and about 10 quests per district (about 52 in all); quests more involved than Lesson 12's.
- **The gate:** the 3 main lessons and 7 of the district's quests open the next district, so a player goes through about
  10 lessons per district and about 40 for the unit.
- **Words:** at most 50 per main lesson, at most 20 per quest; just under 1,000 words met, a strong grasp of about 500.
- **Day and night:** only when an event calls for it.
- **Systems:** no Stardew systems beyond the open world itself; trust storylines with characters.
